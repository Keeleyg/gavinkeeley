## About

I've lived my life with a single purpose: "to achieve what is thought to be impossible". It's this purpose that led me to write *Lead on Purpose*, and it's the same purpose that has driven four decades of challenging conventional wisdom about how organisations should be led.

I've spent those forty years watching leaders fail in predictable ways. Not because they lacked intelligence or ambition, but because they were never actually trained to lead. They were promoted, handed teams, and left to wing it. I wrote this book because I got tired of staying quiet about it.

My approach to leadership is built on a simple premise: evidence first, values always, dogma never. In a world where classical management theory has expired, where Friedman's doctrine is dead, and where leaders have days to respond rather than months, we need an entirely different foundation. That foundation rests on five values: Curiosity, Humility, Action, Care, and Velocity.

My perspective comes from four decades of technology and business leadership across four continents. I've held executive roles at Suncorp, Aviva PLC, and Hollard Insurance, and have founded and led multiple technology companies globally. My scientific training established an evidence-based approach to decision-making and a commitment to holding strong views loosely.

Today, I'm Partner at Circle Leadership Global and Tractor Ventures, focusing on leadership development and venture capital. I advise organisations of all sizes, holding multiple board positions, as well as being an Adjunct Professor at Queensland University of Technology.

Achieving the impossible isn't just philosophy. I led a seven-year campaign for Queensland's undersea fibre optic connection that many said couldn't be done. I served as inaugural Chair of Regional Development Australia for City of Moreton Bay, Sunshine Coast, and Noosa—a federal ministerial appointment in the region I've called home with my family for the last 20 years.

I've been inducted into both the Queensland Pearcey Entrepreneur Hall of Fame and the Australian Computer Society Hall of Fame.

I remain convinced that leadership can be learned, that most of what passes for leadership wisdom is comfortable fiction, and that the leaders dominating today's headlines are definitely not examples to follow.

> "You are currently experiencing the fastest change in your life, but it will be the slowest you experience for the rest of your lifetime. How prepared do you feel to lead through that?"
